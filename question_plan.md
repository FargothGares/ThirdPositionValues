PRACTICAL AXIS: Insurrection vs Reform
CLASS AXIS: Domination vs Collaboration
DIPLOMATIC AXIS: Isolation vs Cooperation
IDENTITY AXIS: Kinship vs Citizenship
CULTURAL AXIS: Transgressive vs Restoration
TECHNOLOGICAL AXIS: Development vs Preservation
SOCIETAL AXIS: Totality vs Plurality
RELIGIOUS AXIS: Sacred vs Profane

INSURRECTION QUESTIONS:
A movement seeking real change must be prepared to act outside the laws of the existing state.
Every system, including the current one, will crush any movement that poses a threat to it.
Gradual reform ultimately serves to preserve the system it claims to change.
The institutions of the existing order cannot be meaningfully transformed without first being overthrown.
Compromise with the existing order ultimately betrays the principles of our movement.

REFORM QUESTIONS:
We must partake in electoral politics in order to take office.
We must take a position of moral superiority over the left, and maintain a peaceful movement.
Lasting political change is best achieved through gradual, peaceful transformation.
Existing political institutions can be used as instruments for achieving fundamental change. 
A movement should demonstrate its ability to govern before attempting to transform society. 

DOMINATION QUESTIONS:
The state must embody one particular social or economic class.
Only one particular class is fit to lead the nation.
The interests of certain class(es) are inherently anti-national, and hence they must be liquidated.
A stable nation requires a clearly defined hierarchy between its social and economic classes.
Political power should belong primarily to the class whose interests are most compatible with the national interest.

COLLABORATION QUESTIONS:
The unity of the nation requires each of its social & economic classes, regardless of their differences, to cooperate.
Talk of class warfare, or the supremacy of one class over another, is only harmful for the nation.
All social and economic classes can adapt to live harmoniously in the new system we are going to build.
The nation is strengthened when dialogue exists between different social and economic classes.
The prosperity of one social or economic class should not come at the deliberate expense of another.

ISOLATION QUESTIONS:
Alliances, even those with ideologically-aligned states, come at the cost of our own sovereignty.
It is in the nation's interests to seek economic self-sufficiency to the highest degree possible.
A 'military-first' principle is essential to ensure our state is left undisturbed by international enemies, far and near.
Dependence on foreign powers, even in matters of mutual interest, ultimately compromises national independence.
Our nation should avoid binding international commitments that could compel it to act against its own interests.

COOPERATION QUESTIONS:
We must work with ideologically-aligned states to build a new geopolitical bloc to challenge the current dominant powers.
A nation can better secure its interests by pooling its strength with friendly nations than by standing alone. 
International trade and economic interdependence can strengthen the nations that participate in them.
Diplomatic and strategic partnerships allow nations to accomplish what they could not accomplish independently.
The interests of allied nations can be sufficiently compatible to justify enduring commitments between them.

KINSHIP QUESTIONS:
The nation is a body of people who are defined primarily by shared ancestry.
Citizenship should not be handed out to those whose ancestors have no tie to these lands.
I share more in common with someone of my ethnic heritage born in another country than I do with someone of a different ethnic heritage born in my country.
Assimilation is a myth, as culture is downstream of genetics.
Immigration from biologically or culturally distant countries destroys nations through admixture.

CITIZENSHIP QUESTIONS:
Anyone who genuinely adopts the nation's laws, institutions, and civic culture can become fully part of the nation.
Legal citizenship should be the primary basis for determining who belongs to the nation.
Belonging to a nation comes from works done for said nation, someone born abroad can assimilate by working for the common good.
A nation can incorporate people from different ancestral backgrounds without losing its essential identity.
Commitment to the nation's political community matters more than inherited characteristics in determining national belonging.

TRANSGRESSIVE QUESTIONS:
Social taboos should be challenged when they stand in the way of cultural renewal.
A healthy culture must be willing to discard traditions that have outlived their usefulness.
Moral conventions inherited from previous generations should not be treated as inherently authoritative.
Art and cultural expression should be free to violate established social norms.
Deliberately challenging established customs can be necessary to create a stronger culture.

REGRESSIVE QUESTIONS:
A society should actively recover customs and traditions that have been abandoned in the modern era.
Long-standing social taboos often preserve wisdom that should not be discarded merely because they are outdated.
Cultural reform should generally restore older standards rather than invent entirely new ones.
Inherited customs deserve preservation even when their original practical purpose is no longer apparent.
Modern society has discarded important traditions that should be deliberately reintroduced.

DEVELOPMENT QUESTIONS:
Technological and industrial development is a necessity for the advancement of the nation.
Adhering to environmental regulations, when many other states do not, is only detrimental to us.
Unused land should be made productive through construction, agriculture, infrastructure, or industry.
Economic growth should take precedence over preserving landscapes in their existing state.
A nation should continually expand its productive capacity rather than settle for what it has already built.

PRESERVATION QUESTIONS:
Some natural landscapes possess an intrinsic value that economic development cannot replace.
Certain areas should remain undeveloped regardless of their potential economic value.
The destruction of ecosystems for short-term economic gain is an unacceptable cost of development.
A nation's natural heritage should be deliberately protected for future generations.
Development should be concentrated where it causes the least disruption to the surrounding natural environment.

TOTALITY QUESTIONS:
All within the state, nothing outside the state, nothing against the state.
The state must possess the authority to subordinate all competing institutions to the national whole.
Political unity requires a common governing doctrine rather than competing ideological factions.
Organised opposition to the established order should not be permitted to develop into an independent political force.
The institutions of civil society should serve the political and national objectives of the state rather than operate independently of them.

PLURALITY QUESTIONS:
Shutting down dialogue between opposing views in our new system will only harm us intellectually.
Different political tendencies should be able to organise and advocate for their interests within the national system.
The nation is strengthened when power is distributed among institutions capable of checking one another.
Regional and local authorities should retain meaningful powers of their own rather than merely executing the decisions of the central government.
Different social organisations can pursue their own interests while remaining part of a common political order.

SACRED QUESTIONS:
Religious values largely inform the rest of a person's belief system.
Religious tradition should have a meaningful place in the moral education of the nation.
A nation benefits from sharing a sacred narrative that gives its people a sense of higher purpose.
Religious institutions should be able to influence the customs and values of society.
Faith can provide forms of moral knowledge that reason alone cannot fully supply.

PROFANE QUESTIONS:
Religion ought to be cast into the dustbin of history.
Moral principles should be grounded in human reason rather than divine revelation.
Public institutions should operate independently of religious authority.
Religious claims should be subjected to the same critical scrutiny as any other claims about reality.
A society can develop a coherent moral order without appealing to supernatural beliefs.
